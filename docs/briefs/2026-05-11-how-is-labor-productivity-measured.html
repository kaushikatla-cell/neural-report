Evidence Brief

How is “labor productivity” actually measured?

Published 2026-05-11 · Authors: Kaushik Atla (research lead), Divyam Gupta (editorial), Shivam Pande (IG) · Read time ~5 min

TL;DR

  • In the U.S., the headline concept is usually output per hour worked—not “how hard people try,” and not the same as corporate profit margins.
  • Different productivity series cover different sectors (nonfarm business is common in news) and can move differently from “the whole economy.”
  • Uncertainty: Quality adjustment, intangible investment, and timing of measurement revisions mean productivity is a serious statistic—not a vibe check for AI tweets.

Claims and evidence

Claim 1: “Labor productivity is a ratio: output ÷ labor hours.”

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, “Productivity and Costs” overview.

What we concluded: BLS defines labor productivity as real output per hour worked for the sector covered by a given series. That definition is why AI can matter in theory (more output per hour) but still be hard to see in aggregates early on.

What would change our mind: A BLS methodology change that redefines the headline numerator/denominator for major series—then we’d revise how we explain “headline productivity.”

Claim 2: “You must read which sector index you’re looking at.”

Source: BLS program documentation linked from the same productivity hub (sector coverage notes).

What we concluded: “Productivity went up” without naming the sector index is a common ambiguity trap—especially when people compare tech firms to national averages.

What would change our mind: N/A—this is definitional discipline, not a forecast claim.

Still uncertain because…

  • AI may raise measured output via quality improvements that are statistically hard to capture quickly.
  • Compositional shifts (who is employed, where hours land) can move averages without changing “true” technology.
