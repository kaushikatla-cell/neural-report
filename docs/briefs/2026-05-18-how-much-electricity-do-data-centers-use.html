Evidence Brief

How much electricity do data centers use—and how much is “because of AI”?

Published 2026-05-18 · Authors: Kaushik Atla (research lead), Divyam Gupta (editorial), Shivam Pande (IG) · Read time ~6 min

TL;DR

  • The IEA’s Energy and AI special report provides a global modeling framework and headline magnitudes for data centre electricity use—useful for policy and infrastructure planning, not for dunking on a single company.
  • Data centres are still a modest share of global electricity in headline percent terms, but can be locally concentrated in ways that stress grids and planning processes.
  • Uncertainty: Scenario ranges (Base / Lift-Off / Headwinds / High Efficiency) are wide by 2035—efficiency gains, adoption curves, and grid bottlenecks all matter.

Claims and evidence

Claim 1: “Global data centre electricity demand is material and growing fast vs. total power demand.”

Source: International Energy Agency, “Energy and AI” (special report), including the executive summary and demand chapter.

What we concluded: The IEA connects AI uptake to accelerated deployment of high-performance servers and projects rising data centre electricity consumption through 2030 under its Base Case—with explicit sensitivity cases that widen the range thereafter.

What would change our mind: A major IEA revision to the underlying global dataset or model assumptions that materially shifts published magnitudes—then we’d update numbers and date-stamp the change.

Claim 2: “AI is a key driver of growth, but not the only digital-services driver.”

Source: IEA, “Energy demand from AI” chapter.

What we concluded: The IEA separates accelerated-server growth (AI-linked) from other data centre demand drivers; still, headline “data centres” bundles multiple customer types and infrastructure loads.

What would change our mind: Empirical disaggregation in official statistics that cleanly splits “AI-only” power draw from non-AI digital demand at national scale—currently scarce.

Still uncertain because…

  • PPAs, on-site generation, and accounting for offsets complicate “who consumes what” stories.
  • Regional grid interconnection queues can delay capacity even when financial commitments exist.

Sources

  2. IEA — Energy demand from AI
